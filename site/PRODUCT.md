# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Day hikers in the Pacific Northwest and beyond who already track hikes (Strava, AllTrails) and want something that remembers which trails were worth it. They land here from a friend's shared flyover video or the App Store, usually on a phone, deciding in under a minute whether to download.

## Product Purpose

Apex is an iPhone app that records a hike, plays it back as a 3D flyover, and ranks it against every trail the hiker has done with quick this-or-that questions. Groups race a weekly climbing board. This page exists to turn a visit into a download: show the real app doing the real thing, fast.

## Brand Personality

Outdoorsy, exact, warm. Feels like a well-made trail map: paper, contour lines, ink, a summit register. Confident, never cute. The data is the decoration (real elevation, real routes, real NPS difficulty).

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS landing pages: gradient blobs, glass cards, hero-metric templates, identical icon-card grids.
- Strava's orange athletic intensity and AllTrails' green-and-white directory feel.
- Editorial-magazine serif affectation.
- Motion for its own sake: fade-in on every section, tilting phones, bouncing chips.

## Design Principles

1. Show the app working, not adjectives about it. Every section carries a live piece of the product.
2. The map is the brand. Paper, contours, the five-step difficulty ramp, distance blue and climb brown, used everywhere they mean something.
3. Motion explains. Things move because a hike is moving (a route drawing, elevation streaming, a list re-sorting), never to decorate a scroll.
4. One idea per screen, plainly worded.
5. Same truth as the app: real trail numbers, real screens, real colors.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Body text at least 4.5:1, controls at least 44px tall, visible focus rings, a skip link. Every animation rests under prefers-reduced-motion, and any loop longer than five seconds can be paused.
